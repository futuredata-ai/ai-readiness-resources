# KPI Architecture Starter Note for SMEs

## Purpose

This starter note helps small and medium-sized enterprises think about key performance indicators before building dashboards, business intelligence reports or AI-enabled decision-support tools.

A KPI architecture is not just a list of numbers. It is a structured connection between business objectives, data sources, reporting needs and decisions.

## 1. What Is a KPI?

A Key Performance Indicator is a measurable signal that helps an organisation understand whether an activity, process or objective is moving in the right direction.

A useful KPI should be:

- Clearly defined
- Connected to a business objective
- Based on reliable data
- Reviewed at the right frequency
- Owned by a responsible person or team
- Linked to a real decision

A KPI is not useful if nobody knows what action should follow from it.

## 2. Common KPI Categories

SMEs can begin by organising KPIs into simple categories.

### Commercial KPIs

Examples:

- Revenue
- Sales growth
- Conversion rate
- Average order value
- Customer acquisition cost
- Customer retention rate

### Operational KPIs

Examples:

- Process completion time
- Delivery time
- Stock availability
- Error rate
- Support response time
- Workflow bottlenecks

### Financial KPIs

Examples:

- Gross margin
- Net profit margin
- Cash flow
- Cost per service
- Monthly recurring revenue
- Budget variance

### Marketing KPIs

Examples:

- Website traffic
- Lead volume
- Cost per lead
- Ad conversion rate
- Email open rate
- Social engagement

### Service Quality KPIs

Examples:

- Customer satisfaction
- Complaint rate
- Repeat purchase rate
- Resolution time
- Service completion accuracy

## 3. KPI Design Questions

Before selecting KPIs, an organisation should ask:

- What decision will this KPI support?
- Which business objective is connected to it?
- What data source will be used?
- Who owns the data?
- How often should the KPI be reviewed?
- What threshold requires action?
- What happens if the KPI improves or declines?

These questions help prevent dashboards from becoming decorative rather than useful.

## 4. Data Sources for KPIs

Common SME data sources include:

- Sales systems
- Accounting records
- CRM tools
- Website analytics
- Advertising platforms
- Customer service records
- Operational spreadsheets
- Inventory systems
- Supplier records
- Manual reporting logs

Each KPI should be mapped to a specific data source. If the data source is unclear, the KPI may not be reliable.

## 5. KPI Ownership

Every important KPI should have an owner.

The owner is responsible for:

- Checking data accuracy
- Reviewing results
- Explaining changes
- Escalating problems
- Suggesting action
- Updating the KPI definition when needed

Without ownership, KPI reporting often becomes inconsistent and weak.

## 6. Reporting Frequency

Not all KPIs should be reviewed at the same frequency.

Possible reporting cycles:

- Daily: operational monitoring
- Weekly: sales, marketing and workflow performance
- Monthly: financial and management review
- Quarterly: strategic performance review
- Annual: long-term planning and investment decisions

The reporting frequency should match the decision cycle.

## 7. KPI Architecture and AI Readiness

AI readiness depends partly on KPI readiness.

Before using AI tools for analysis, prediction or automation, an organisation should know:

- Which outcomes matter
- Which indicators measure those outcomes
- Which data sources are reliable
- Which patterns require human review
- Which decisions should remain under human oversight

AI cannot compensate for unclear objectives, weak data or poorly defined performance indicators.

## 8. Starter KPI Architecture Template

A simple KPI architecture can be organised as follows:

| Business Objective | KPI | Data Source | Owner | Frequency | Decision Supported |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Improve sales visibility | Monthly revenue | Sales records | Sales lead | Monthly | Revenue planning |
| Improve marketing efficiency | Cost per lead | Ad platform | Marketing lead | Weekly | Campaign optimisation |
| Improve service quality | Response time | Support records | Operations lead | Weekly | Service improvement |
| Improve financial control | Budget variance | Accounting records | Finance lead | Monthly | Cost management |

## 9. Practical First Steps

An SME can begin with the following actions:

- List the main business objectives.
- Select 5 to 10 priority KPIs.
- Define each KPI clearly.
- Connect each KPI to a data source.
- Assign an owner.
- Decide the reporting frequency.
- Create a simple reporting table or dashboard.
- Review whether each KPI supports a real decision.
- Remove indicators that do not lead to action.
- Reassess the KPI structure before adopting advanced BI or AI tools.
