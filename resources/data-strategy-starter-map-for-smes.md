# Data Strategy Starter Map for SMEs

## Purpose

This starter map is designed to help small and medium-sized enterprises understand the basic building blocks of a practical data strategy before investing in advanced analytics, business intelligence tools or AI-enabled systems.

Many organisations want to adopt AI or automation but do not yet have a clear view of their data sources, reporting needs, decision-making processes or operational priorities. This resource provides a simple structure for identifying what should be clarified first.

## 1. Business Objectives

Before collecting more data or adopting new tools, an organisation should define what it wants to improve.

Key questions:

- What business decisions need better evidence?
- Which operations are currently unclear, slow or difficult to monitor?
- Which departments or workflows would benefit from better reporting?
- What problems should data help solve?
- Are the objectives operational, financial, commercial, compliance-related or strategic?

Examples of useful objectives:

- Better sales visibility
- More consistent KPI reporting
- Improved customer segmentation
- Better stock or demand planning
- Clearer financial monitoring
- More structured operational dashboards
- More informed AI readiness planning

## 2. Data Sources

A basic data strategy should identify where useful data already exists.

Common data sources include:

- Sales records
- Customer enquiries
- Website analytics
- CRM data
- Accounting data
- Inventory or stock data
- Email and communication records
- Operational spreadsheets
- Supplier information
- Marketing campaign data
- Customer support requests

For each data source, the organisation should ask:

- Who owns this data?
- Where is it stored?
- Is it updated regularly?
- Is it reliable?
- Is it complete?
- Can it be exported or connected to other systems?

## 3. Data Quality

Data quality is essential before using dashboards, automation or AI tools.

Basic data quality checks:

- Are records complete?
- Are there duplicate entries?
- Are names, dates, categories and values consistent?
- Are important fields missing?
- Is the data updated frequently enough?
- Is there a clear owner responsible for corrections?
- Are sensitive or personal data fields handled carefully?

Poor data quality can lead to misleading reports, weak KPI monitoring and unreliable AI or automation outcomes.

## 4. KPI Structure

A useful data strategy should connect data to measurable indicators.

Possible KPI categories:

- Sales and revenue indicators
- Customer acquisition indicators
- Customer retention indicators
- Operational efficiency indicators
- Cost and profitability indicators
- Marketing performance indicators
- Service quality indicators
- Risk and compliance indicators

Each KPI should have:

- A clear definition
- A data source
- A reporting frequency
- A responsible owner
- A business decision connected to it

A KPI is useful only if it supports a real decision.

## 5. Reporting and Business Intelligence

Business intelligence should help managers and teams understand performance clearly.

Before creating dashboards, the organisation should define:

- Who needs the report?
- What decisions will the report support?
- How often should it be updated?
- Which KPIs should be included?
- Which data sources are required?
- What level of detail is useful?
- What should trigger action?

Good reporting is not about showing more charts. It is about making decisions easier, faster and more reliable.

## 6. AI Readiness Connection

AI readiness depends on data readiness.

Before adopting AI tools, an organisation should ask:

- Is the data reliable enough?
- Are the business objectives clear?
- Are the processes documented?
- Are there measurable KPIs?
- Are there data privacy or compliance risks?
- Is there internal capacity to review AI-generated outputs?
- Is human oversight built into the process?

AI should not be treated as a shortcut for unclear processes or poor data structure.

## 7. Practical Starter Checklist

An SME can begin with the following actions:

- List the main business decisions that need better information.
- Identify the most important data sources.
- Check whether the data is complete and consistent.
- Define 5–10 priority KPIs.
- Map each KPI to a business objective.
- Decide who owns each data source.
- Create a simple reporting structure.
- Review risks related to personal, sensitive or confidential data.
- Identify one or two realistic AI or automation use cases.
- Build a short roadmap before investing in tools.

## 8. Recommended First Roadmap

A simple first roadmap may include:

### Phase 1: Data Inventory

Identify available data sources, owners and current storage methods.

### Phase 2: Data Quality Review

Check completeness, consistency, duplication and reliability.

### Phase 3: KPI Design

Define the most important indicators and connect them to decisions.

### Phase 4: Reporting Structure

Create simple reports or dashboards that support real business needs.

### Phase 5: AI Readiness Review

Assess whether the organisation is ready to use AI tools responsibly and effectively.
