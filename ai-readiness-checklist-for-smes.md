# AI Readiness Checklist for SMEs

A practical checklist for small and medium-sized enterprises that want to evaluate whether they are ready to adopt AI-enabled tools, data-driven workflows and business intelligence practices.

## 1. Business Objective

Before adopting AI, an organisation should define the business problem it wants to solve.

Key questions:

- What decision or process do we want to improve?
- Is the problem operational, financial, marketing-related, customer-related or strategic?
- Can the expected benefit be measured?
- Is AI necessary, or would better data organisation already solve part of the problem?

## 2. Data Availability

AI and business intelligence depend on usable data.

Key questions:

- What data do we already collect?
- Is the data stored in spreadsheets, CRM systems, accounting tools, e-commerce platforms or other software?
- Is the data complete, structured and regularly updated?
- Are there duplicate, missing or inconsistent records?
- Who is responsible for maintaining the data?

## 3. Data Quality

Poor data quality leads to poor analysis and unreliable AI outputs.

Key questions:

- Are data fields clearly defined?
- Are naming conventions consistent?
- Are records regularly checked for errors?
- Is there a process for correcting inaccurate information?
- Can the organisation trust its current reports?

## 4. KPI Architecture

AI readiness requires clear performance indicators.

Key questions:

- Which KPIs matter most for the business?
- Are KPIs linked to real decisions?
- Are KPIs reviewed weekly, monthly or quarterly?
- Are dashboards available to decision-makers?
- Are indicators aligned with business strategy?

## 5. Process Readiness

AI should support structured processes, not replace unclear workflows.

Key questions:

- Are key business processes documented?
- Are responsibilities clearly assigned?
- Are there repetitive tasks that could be automated?
- Are manual processes creating delays or errors?
- Is there a clear approval process for operational changes?

## 6. Digital Tools

The organisation should understand its existing technology environment.

Key questions:

- What software tools are currently used?
- Are systems connected or fragmented?
- Can data be exported and analysed?
- Are staff trained to use existing tools properly?
- Are there security or access-control issues?

## 7. Human Oversight

AI adoption should include human review, accountability and responsible use.

Key questions:

- Who will review AI-generated outputs?
- Who is responsible for final decisions?
- Are employees trained to understand the limits of AI?
- Are sensitive or personal data protected?
- Is there a policy for responsible AI use?

## 8. Implementation Priority

AI readiness should start with small, measurable and low-risk use cases.

Good first use cases may include:

- Reporting automation
- KPI dashboards
- Customer segmentation
- Market intelligence summaries
- Internal knowledge organisation
- Sales or operational forecasting
- Document classification and workflow support

## 9. Initial Readiness Score

A simple first assessment can use the following scale:

- 0 = Not ready
- 1 = Early preparation needed
- 2 = Partially ready
- 3 = Ready for a small pilot
- 4 = Ready for structured implementation

Recommended dimensions:

- Business objective clarity
- Data availability
- Data quality
- KPI structure
- Process maturity
- Digital tool maturity
- Human oversight
- Security and compliance awareness

## 10. Next Step

An SME should not begin with complex AI systems. The recommended first step is to map its data sources, define its key decisions and create a basic KPI and reporting structure.

AI readiness starts with organised information, clear objectives and responsible decision-making.
